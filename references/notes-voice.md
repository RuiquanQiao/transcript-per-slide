# Writing `notes`

The student should feel like the lecturer is explaining this slide to them, and
it should read faster than listening would.

## Voice

**First person, the lecturer talking to the student**: *I*, *we*, *you*.

| Write | Not |
|---|---|
| "I'll show you why this loop is slow." | "The lecturer then explains why the loop is slow." |
| "Don't email your submission; upload it on the course site." | "He reminds students not to email submissions." |

Never narrate in third person, and never address the reader as "the student".

## Cleaning up speech

- Drop fillers and restarts: *uh, um, like, you know, right?, OK so, sort of*.
- Fix recognition errors from context (*cash* → cache). If you
  cannot tell what was said, write what is certain and leave the rest out.
  Never guess a number.
- Keep every number, date, time, percentage, and name exactly as said.
- Keep the lecturer's own examples, analogies and warnings. They are why
  reading this beats reading the slides.
- Keep the jokes that teach something. Drop the ones that only fill time.

## Shape

- Short paragraphs and bullets. Bold the one or two things to remember.
- Code, formulas and anything with `*` go in backticks or code blocks
  (Markdown eats `x*y*z` as emphasis).
- Do not repeat the slide text back. The image is right above. Add what the
  lecturer said *about* it.
- Length follows the speech: a slide talked about for five minutes gets more
  than one shown for ten seconds.

## Do not

- Not a filler dump, not a third-person summary (`he continues`, `the lecturer
  said`), not a new essay.
- Do not paste raw transcript into `pack.md`, even cleaned of fillers. Rewrite it.
- Do not add teaching the lecturer did not do. That is what tutoring blocks are
  for, and only when the student asks.
- Do not soften or hedge announcements. "The deadline is hard" stays hard.

## Example

Raw segments:

> so uh the thing is, right, every time you, every time you look at the middle
> you're throwing away, you know, half of what's left, so from a million it's
> like, what, twenty looks, basically, that's it

Notes:

> Every look at the middle throws away **half** of what is left. From a million
> items, that is about **20 looks**.
